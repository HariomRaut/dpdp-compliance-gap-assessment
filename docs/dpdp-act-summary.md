# DPDP Act 2023 – Key Principles (Summary)

## 1. Scope & Applicability
- Applies to processing of personal data where the data fiduciary is located in India or where the processing is in connection with any business carried on in India.
- Covers both digital and non‑digital personal data.

## 2. Definitions (Selected)
- **Data Principal**: The individual to whom the personal data relates.
- **Data Fiduciary**: Entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.
- **Personal Data**: Any data relating to an identifiable natural person.
- **Sensitive Personal Data**: Includes financial data, health data, biometric data, caste/tribe, religious or political beliefs, sexual orientation, etc.

## 3. Obligations of Data Fiduciary
| Obligation | Description | Practical Control |
|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Notice & Consent** | Provide a clear, concise notice in plain language; obtain free, informed, specific, and unambiguous consent before processing. | Consent checkbox with timestamp; retain consent receipts. |
| **Purpose Limitation** | Process data only for the specific purpose(s) disclosed in the notice. | Purpose‑binding metadata; audit logs to verify no secondary use. |
| **Data Minimisation** | Collect only the data necessary for the disclosed purpose. | Form validation to reject unnecessary fields; data‑field classification. |
| **Storage Limitation** | Retain personal data only as long as necessary for the purpose; delete or anonymise thereafter. | Retention schedules; automated purge jobs. |
| **Data Principal Rights** | Rights to access, correction, erasure, portability, and grievance redressal. | Self‑service portal for DSR (Data Subject Requests); workflow to fulfil requests within stipulated timelines. |
| **Data Breach Notification** | Notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected data principals in case of a breach likely to cause harm. | Incident‑response plan; breach detection logs; notification templates. |
| **Accountability & Governance** | Maintain records of processing activities; conduct DPIA where high‑risk processing is envisaged. | RoPA maintenance; DPIA checklist and reporting. |

## 4. Penalties
- Up to ₹250 crore or 2% of global turnover (whichever is higher) for non‑compliance with certain provisions.
- Up to ₹500 crore or 4% of global turnover for violations concerning children’s data or failure to implement reasonable security safeguards.

## 5. Relationship with Other Frameworks
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022**: Provides a systematic approach to managing information security; many controls map directly to DPDP obligations (e.g., A.8.2 for privilege management, A.9.4 for access control, A.12.6 for vulnerability management).
- **GDPR**: DPDP draws heavily from GDPR principles; familiarity with GDPR eases compliance.
